Contamination Screen

A visual guide to the logic and design of contamination_screen.py — a pairwise cross-sample contamination detector for haematological oncology panel VCFs.

Author Joo Wook Ahn Date 10 May 2026 Tool contamination_screen.py (1,009 lines) Pipeline Uranus (eggd_vep output)

01 Overview

This tool detects cross-sample DNA contamination in a batch of tumour VCFs from the same sequencing run. It works by comparing every pair of samples and looking for variants that appear at a consistent, diluted ratio — the hallmark of one sample’s DNA leaking into another.

What does it do?

For every pair of samples (A, B) on a run:

  1. Find all variants present in both VCFs (shared variants)
  2. Compute the VAF ratio: VAF_B / VAF_A for each shared variant
  3. Build a histogram of log2(ratio) and look for peaks
  4. If a peak exists away from ratio=1, flag it as contamination
Key concept

This tool is fundamentally different from somatic variant calling. Contamination detection uses germline variants as markers — the very variants that clinical reporting pipelines filter out. The tool intentionally retains common germline SNPs because they are the most reliable contamination signal.

02 The Contamination Signal

When sample A’s DNA contaminates sample B, the source’s germline heterozygous variants (at ~50% VAF in source) appear at a diluted, consistent fraction in the recipient:

VAFrecipient ≈ VAFsource × contamination_fraction

For example, if 25% of B’s library is actually from A, then A’s germline het variants (~50% VAF) will appear in B at ~12.5% VAF. The ratio across all these markers is consistently ~0.25:

log2(VAFB / VAFA) = log2(0.125 / 0.50) = log2(0.25) = −2.0

03 Pre-filtering Pipeline

Before comparing pairs, each VCF is pre-filtered with a 4-stage bcftools pipeline. The goal is to keep only variants where VAF is reliably measured and remove one specific population-frequency artefact.

Why filter gnomAD AF ≥ 0.40?

At high population frequencies, many individuals are homozygous-alt (AF ≈ 1.0) while others are heterozygous (AF ≈ 0.5). The ratio between them is exactly 2:1, which mimics 50% contamination.

FilterThresholdRationale
PASS onlySentieon TNfilterRemoves strand bias, weak evidence, etc.
Read depthDP ≥ 99At DP < 99, VAF noise is too high for reliable ratios
VAF floorAF ≥ 0.03Below 3%, allele fractions are unreliable
gnomAD AF< 0.40Removes hom/het 2:1 artefact (see above)
Design decision

No other population-frequency filter is applied. Clinical filters like gnomAD AF ≥ 0.002 would destroy 87% of the contamination signal. This tool deliberately retains common germline variants because they ARE the signal.

04 Dual-Peak Detection

Because we keep common germline variants, two unrelated people will share many variants at ratio ≈ 1 (both carry them as hets at ~50% VAF). The contamination signal appears as a second peak at a different ratio. The algorithm finds both:

The unity zone boundary: |log2| ≤ 0.3

The boundary between “unity” (shared germline) and “non-unity” (potential contamination) is set at |log2| = 0.3, corresponding to a ratio range of 0.81–1.23. This captures measurement noise at typical panel depths:

Read depthSE(log2 ratio)3σ rangeCaptured by 0.3?

At typical panel depth (1000x), essentially all germline noise falls within the unity zone. Contamination at fractions up to 81% produces peaks outside this zone and is detected.

Detection range

The tool detects contamination fractions from ~3% (limited by VAF floor) up to ~81%. Above 81%, the signal falls inside the unity zone but manifests as a near-complete sample swap — captured by the overall peak instead.

05 Flagging Thresholds

A pair is flagged only when all three conditions are met simultaneously:

peak_count ≥ 10
Non-unity peak strength

The non-unity peak must contain at least 10 variants from independent genomic loci, all at a consistent non-unity ratio. This ensures the signal is not from a few linked variants.

Default: 10
n_shared_z ≥ 2.0
Within-run z-score of n_shared

The pair must share more variants than typical for this run. Contamination increases shared variant count because source variants appear in the recipient.

Default: 2.0
FREEMIX ≥ 15%
VerifyBamID cross-contamination estimate

When a FREEMIX file is provided, the recipient’s VerifyBamID estimate must also be elevated. This removes somatic artefacts where the BAM is clean but VCF ratios appear suspicious.

Optional (requires --freemix-file)
n_shared ≥ 10
Minimum shared variants

At least 10 shared variants must exist to even assess the pair. With fewer, ratio statistics are unreliable.

Pre-condition

Why two thresholds?

Each threshold catches a different class of false positive:

  • peak_count alone would flag somatic LOH samples (cancer driver genes at AF=1.0 create false peaks of 4–8 variants). The z-score requirement catches these because total variant sharing is not elevated.
  • n_shared_z alone would flag samples that happen to share many common germline variants (high n_shared) without any ratio clustering. The peak_count requirement catches these.
Linkage disequilibrium

Multiple variants in the same gene on the same haplotype (e.g. 5 PRPF8 SNPs) produce linked false peaks of 4–5 variants. These fall below the peak_count threshold of 10. Genuine contamination produces 10–28 variants from multiple independent loci spanning different chromosomes.

Within-run z-scoring

The z-score of n_shared is always computed within a single run (all pairs from that sequencing batch), never globally. This is critical because:

  • Newer VEP config (gnomADg + gnomADe): mean n_shared ≈ 19, SD ≈ 4.9
  • Older VEP config (gnomADe only): mean n_shared ≈ 8, SD ≈ 2.9
  • A global SD would blend these populations and produce biased z-scores in both directions

06 Known False Positives

The retrospective analysis identified four patterns that can produce false or misleading signals. Reviewers should understand these when interpreting results:

Somatic LOH
Cancer driver genes at AF ≈ 1.0

High-purity tumours with loss-of-heterozygosity show somatic variants at AF=1.0. Any unrelated het sample produces log2(0.5/1.0) = −1.0 exactly. Diagnostic: same sample is “source” against many others, all at ratio exactly 0.5; FREEMIX normal.

Caught by FREEMIX filter
Bone Marrow Chimerism
Post-transplant mixed DNA

Post-HSCT patients have mixed donor/recipient DNA — mechanistically identical to contamination. Diagnostic: reproducible across runs of same patient; FREEMIX 18–47%; appears as recipient vs many sources.

Indistinguishable from contamination in VCF
Engineered Cell Lines
Control samples with synthetic variants

Cell line controls carry engineered variants that produce the highest signals in any cohort (peak=28, nz=13.45). Mitigation: exclude controls with --exclude "*Q*" via dx_fetch.

Exclude from screening
Transitive Contamination
Secondary signal from heavily contaminated sample

If Y is 50% contaminated by Z, Y now carries Z’s variants at ~25% VAF. Other samples sharing Z’s germline may flag against Y. Diagnostic: Y is recipient in high-confidence pairs AND spurious source in weaker pairs.

07 FREEMIX Correlation

VerifyBamID FREEMIX estimates contamination from the BAM at germline SNP positions. The two tools are complementary, not redundant:

Detects recipients✓ Sensitive✓ Via non-unity peak
Identifies source✗✓ Directionality from ratio sign
Estimates fraction✓ (FREEMIX %)✓ (from peak position)
Distinguishes primary/transitive✗✓ From pair topology
Works at low levels (<5%)✓Limited (few markers)
Plate-position awareness✗✓ (matrix ordering)
Key insight

Sources have near-normal FREEMIX (~0.3%) because their own library is clean. The contamination screen is the only tool that identifies them — FREEMIX cannot tell you where the contamination came from.

FREEMIX threshold rationale

15% was chosen because: all confirmed contamination/chimerism had FREEMIX 18.7–47%. A 20% threshold would miss 2 known chimeric samples (18.7%, 18.8%). The gap between 18.7% (lowest chimeric) and 11% (next uncertain) gives comfortable margin without adding noise from the 5–11% range.

10 Interpretation Guide

When reviewing output, use this decision framework:

Inspecting detail TSVs for borderline cases

When a pair is borderline, check the per-variant detail file:

  • Multi-chromosome signal: variants spanning ≥ 3 chromosomes = contamination (independent loci)
  • Single-gene cluster: 4–5 variants all in one gene (e.g. PRPF8) = linkage disequilibrium (false positive)
  • Source AF ≈ 1.0: if the “source” sample has AF near 1.0 at in-peak positions = somatic LOH, not contamination
  • Source AF ≈ 0.5: typical germline het = genuine contamination signal

The contamination matrix

The matrix.tsv is an N×N grid where rows are recipients and columns are sources. The value in each cell is the peak_count from the non-unity peak. When ordered by plate position (--plate-layout), adjacent-well contamination appears as non-zero entries near the diagonal.

Reviewer tip

Open matrix.tsv in Excel and apply conditional formatting (colour scale) to quickly spot contamination patterns. Adjacent non-zero entries along a row indicate one sample contaminating multiple neighbours. A column with many non-zero values indicates a widespread source.
